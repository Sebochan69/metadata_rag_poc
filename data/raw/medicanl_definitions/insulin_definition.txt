INSULIN - Medical Definition

Insulin is a peptide hormone produced by beta cells in the pancreatic 
islets of Langerhans. It is the primary hormone responsible for 
regulating blood glucose levels.

FUNCTION:
Insulin lowers blood glucose concentration by:
1. Promoting glucose uptake into muscle and adipose tissue
2. Stimulating glycogen synthesis in liver and muscle
3. Inhibiting gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis
4. Promoting protein synthesis and lipogenesis

SECRETION:
Insulin is secreted in response to elevated blood glucose levels, 
particularly after meals. Secretion is also stimulated by amino acids, 
fatty acids, and gastrointestinal hormones.

CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE:
- Deficiency causes Type 1 diabetes mellitus
- Resistance causes Type 2 diabetes mellitus
- Measured clinically to diagnose diabetes and monitor treatment

RELATED CONCEPTS:
- Glucagon (antagonist hormone)
- Blood glucose regulation
- Pancreatic function
- Diabetes mellitus
"""
